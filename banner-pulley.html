Banner Pulley System for the UVA Small Special Collections Library

A safer way to hang the library's large vinyl banners without a 16-foot ladder and without modifying a historic building, developed with the client, UVA Facilities and the Architect's Office.

Term
Spring 2025
Team
5 engineers
Methods
Client interviews, decision matrix, scale prototyping

01Problem

Maintenance workers hung the library's banners by climbing a 16-foot ladder and hooking metal rods to eyelets on the columns, a real fall risk. In wind, the hooks could be overloaded and the banner could come down on people below.

  • No ladder, and safe to operate for staff with a range of physical abilities
  • Withstand year-round weather and wind loads
  • No major structural modifications to a historic building
  • Budget limit of $700

02Approach

We scoped the problem through client and stakeholder interviews, reviewed pulley mechanics and banner wind loading, and worked through the social, technical, environmental and economic constraints. Mechanical systems were chosen over automated ones to reduce tampering and weather damage.

Five concepts (two pulley variants, magnets, a curtain system and a guided track) were scored in a weighted decision matrix on stable support, resistance to external force, simple hanging, simple mechanism and banner clarity. The curtain and track concepts tied at 87/100, and elements of the top designs were combined into a pulley-and-track system.

03Iteration & results

When parts were delayed, we tested on a 1:10 scale 3D-printed model with the client. It passed on non-invasive mounting, easier access and tamper resistance, but string derailed from the open pulleys, so we switched to cased pulleys.

Institutional feedback then reshaped the design: drilling into the columns wasn't allowed, and UVA Facilities found cleat mounting too invasive for the historic structure. The final design is a dual-chain halyard system: weather-resistant chains on either side of the columns, with a pole through the banner's top sleeve secured to each chain by steel wire, so staff raise and lower the banner from the ground.

Because of shipping delays, time and the lack of formal approval from the Architect's Office, the full-scale system wasn't built or installed. UVA Facilities approved the concept, and the design is documented for future implementation at the library or other UVA buildings.
